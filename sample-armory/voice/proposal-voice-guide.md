# Aberdeen Proposal Voice

The test for every sentence: would a senior partner say this out loud to a client
CFO without wincing?

## Principles

1. **Lead with their problem, not our history.** The first paragraph of any
   section is about the client. Aberdeen appears in the second.
2. **Specific beats impressive.** "Reduced month-end close from 14 days to 6"
   beats "drove significant efficiency gains" every time.
3. **Every claim carries a proof point.** If there is no named engagement behind
   it, it is not a claim — it is a hope. Cut it or flag it.
4. **Name the risk first.** Proposals that acknowledge the hard part read as
   competent. Proposals that pretend there isn't one read as inexperienced.
5. **Short sentences under pressure.** Evaluators are reading eleven of these.
6. **Write to the evaluation criteria.** If the RFP weights approach at 40%,
   approach gets the space — regardless of what we find interesting.

## Banned openers

Do not begin a section with any of these:

- "In today's rapidly evolving [anything]"
- "As a trusted partner..."
- "With over N years of experience..."
- "We are excited about the opportunity to..."
- "Our team of highly skilled professionals..."

## Banned constructions

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "leverage our expertise" | say what the expertise actually is |
| "best-in-class methodology" | name the method and where it worked |
| "seamless integration" | describe the integration and its failure mode |
| "world-class team" | name the roles and the people |
| "significant savings" | the number, or the range, or nothing |
| "we believe" | either we know, or we will validate it by date X |

## Structure conventions

- **Headings mirror the client's words.** If they say "Transition Approach",
  the heading is "Transition Approach" — not "Our Transition Philosophy".
- **Lead each section with the answer**, then the reasoning. Evaluators skim.
- **Tables for anything comparative.** Prose for anything persuasive.
- **Executive summary is written last** and reads as though written first.

## Handling thin evidence

Where Aberdeen cannot support a claim from experience, the honest construction
is a commitment with a date:

> "We will validate the current denial-rate baseline in the first two weeks and
> confirm the improvement target before committing to it."

Never:

> "We routinely achieve 30%+ denial reductions."

## Tone by section

| Section | Register |
|---|---|
| Executive summary | Confident, client-first, short paragraphs |
| Approach | Precise and sequential — this is a plan, not a pitch |
| Credentials | Factual. Numbers and outcomes, minimal adjectives |
| Team | Named people, real roles, actual availability |
| Risk | Direct. Name it, own it, mitigate it |
| Commercials | Plain. Explain what drives the number |
