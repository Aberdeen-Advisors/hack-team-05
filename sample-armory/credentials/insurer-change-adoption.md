# Commercial Insurer — Organisational Change & Adoption Programme

## Situation

A commercial insurer had spent $18M on a new underwriting platform that was
technically live and functionally ignored. Nine months after go-live, 38% of
underwriters were still working in the legacy system through an exception
process that had quietly become the norm. Leadership framed it as a training
problem. It was not — the new workflow added steps for the highest-volume
submission type, and nobody had told the project that.

## What Aberdeen did

- Ran an adoption diagnostic rather than accepting the training diagnosis:
  observed 26 underwriters working, measured actual task times in both systems
- Found and quantified the three workflow regressions that made the new platform
  slower for standard submissions, and got them onto the platform backlog with
  business cases attached
- Redesigned the change approach around the underwriter's day rather than around
  the release schedule
- Built role-specific training — four distinct paths, not one deck — and
  delivered it through respected peers rather than the project team
- Established an adoption measurement dashboard tracking system-of-record usage
  by team, reviewed weekly with underwriting leadership
- Ran a structured feedback loop so the next two releases fixed things
  underwriters had actually named

## Outcome

- Legacy exception usage fell from 38% to 4% within five months
- Standard submission handling time in the new platform improved 22% after the
  workflow fixes
- Underwriter satisfaction with the platform moved from 2.4 to 3.9 on a 5-point
  internal survey
- The adoption dashboard was retained by the client as standing operational
  reporting

## Why this is relevant to change pursuits

Use this when an RFP frames a change problem as a training problem. The story
that lands with evaluators is that Aberdeen challenged the client's own diagnosis
and was right — that reads as expertise rather than compliance.
