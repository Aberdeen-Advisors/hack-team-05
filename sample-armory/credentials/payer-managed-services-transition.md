# Health Payer — Application Managed Services Transition

## Situation

A regional health plan was ending a managed services contract with an incumbent
provider on poor terms. Knowledge was concentrated in four incumbent staff, the
run-book was three years stale, and the incumbent had little incentive to
cooperate with a transition. Claims adjudication support and provider portal
maintenance could not be interrupted — a lapse would have shown up in member
service within days.

## What Aberdeen did

- Ran a structured knowledge capture programme in the eight-week window the
  contract allowed: shadowing, recorded walkthroughs, and a rebuilt run-book
  validated by having Aberdeen staff execute it under observation
- Designed a phased transition — monitoring first, then L2 support, then L3 and
  enhancements — so no single cutover carried the whole risk
- Established an SLA framework tied to member-facing outcomes rather than ticket
  counts
- Built a defect and enhancement backlog with the client, prioritised by member
  impact
- Committed to a 6% year-on-year efficiency target across the three-year run,
  with the mechanism written into the contract

## Outcome

- Transition completed with zero missed SLAs during the cutover window
- Run-book rebuilt from 40% coverage to 94% of supported processes
- Ticket resolution time improved 34% by month nine
- Year-one efficiency commitment of 6% met and reported against a measured
  baseline
- Contract extended into a fourth year ahead of term

## Why this is relevant to managed services pursuits

The transition risk story is the differentiator here. Most managed services
proposals treat transition as a formality; evaluators who have lived through a
bad one score heavily on it. Lead with the knowledge-capture method and the
phased cutover, not with the run-state SLA table.
