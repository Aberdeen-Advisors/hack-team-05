# Aberdeen proposal spine

The section order Aberdeen uses for a full proposal response.

This is not invented. It was extracted from a real proposal Aberdeen wrote and sent,
then checked in here so the shape survives without keeping the client's documents in
the repository. Editing the numbered list below changes the spine everywhere: the
proposal outline, the draft response, and the `get_proposal_spine` MCP tool all read it.

**The client's required structure always wins.** Where a solicitation demands different
sections, different names, or a different order, follow the client and put their words
in their positions. Use this spine for everything they left open, and to catch sections
they forgot to ask for but every evaluator expects.

## Section spine

1. Response to Request for Proposal
2. Executive Summary
3. Firm Qualifications & Experience
4. Proposed Approach & Methodology
5. Required Deliverables
6. Team Composition
7. References
8. Proposed Timeline
9. Cost Proposal
10. Additional Differentiators
11. Assumptions, Dependencies & Engagement Terms
12. AI Concierge for This Proposal

## Why the order is what it is

**Executive Summary sits second, never last.** Evaluators read it first and often score
before they finish the document. It is written last and placed early.

**Qualifications come before approach.** The reader decides whether to believe the
approach based on whether the firm has done it before. Approach first reads as theory.

**Deliverables are their own section, not a bullet inside approach.** Procurement scores
against a deliverables list; burying it costs points that have nothing to do with quality.

**References are named, with outcomes.** A reference section without measurable results is
a list of logos. If the corpus cannot support a metric, the section says `[EVIDENCE NEEDED]`
rather than rounding up.

**Assumptions and dependencies are protective, not decorative.** This is where scope
boundaries, client obligations, and the conditions on the price actually live. A proposal
that wins on ambiguous scope loses on delivery.

**The AI Concierge section is an Aberdeen differentiator.** It offers the evaluation
committee a way to interrogate the proposal itself. Keep it last; it is a closing note,
not a capability claim.

## Deck variant

For orals and executive discussion documents, collapse to: the client's problem, what we
heard, approach, team, proof, timeline, commercials, and what happens next. The document
spine above still governs the written leave-behind.
