# Win Theme Patterns

A win theme is not a capability statement. It is a reason to choose Aberdeen,
expressed in the client's terms, backed by a proof point, with the consequence
spelled out for the evaluator.

## The structure that works

> **[Client's problem in their words]** → **[What Aberdeen does differently]** →
> **[Named proof point]** → **[So what for the evaluator]**

**Weak:** "Aberdeen has extensive ERP implementation experience."

**Strong:** "Your last ERP attempt paused at month nine with no go-live. We hold
the systems integrator to a rebaselined plan instead of absorbing their scope —
which is how the regional health system in our credentials went live on date
with no Sev-1 defects. You get an independent party accountable for the date,
not another vendor with an incentive to move it."

## Recurring discriminators

### 1. Independence from the systems integrator
Aberdeen does not act as both SI and assurance provider on the same programme.
Use where an RFP asks for oversight, QA, or programme assurance. Proof:
Regional Health System ERP Modernisation.

### 2. We say the unwelcome thing
Aberdeen presents findings the client did not want, to the audience that needs
them. Use where the RFP hints at a troubled programme or a political
environment. Proof: Regional Bank board audit committee readout.

### 3. Capability transfer as a deliverable, not a courtesy
Aberdeen coaches the client's own leader through the work rather than replacing
them. Use where the client fears vendor dependency. Proof: Regional Bank
programme director retained post-recovery.

### 4. We diagnose before we agree
Where a client has already named their problem, Aberdeen tests the diagnosis.
Use where the stated ask looks like a symptom. Proof: Commercial Insurer —
adoption problem misdiagnosed as a training problem.

### 5. Transition risk taken seriously
For managed services, the transition is the risk, not the run state. Proof:
Health Payer AMS transition with zero missed SLAs from a hostile incumbent.

### 6. Commitments carry a measurement baseline
Aberdeen will not commit to a benefits number without an agreed baseline — and
says so in the proposal. Reads as discipline, not hedging.

## Executive summary skeleton

1. **Their situation, in their words** — two or three sentences. No Aberdeen yet.
2. **What is actually at stake** — the consequence of getting it wrong.
3. **Our understanding of the real problem** — where we differ from the surface ask.
4. **Approach in three moves** — one sentence each.
5. **Why Aberdeen** — two or three win themes, each with its proof point.
6. **What you get** — the specific outcome, named.

Never open with Aberdeen's founding, size, or client count.

## Compliance discipline

- Answer in the RFP's section order unless it explicitly permits otherwise
- Repeat the evaluation criterion at the head of each major section
- Every "shall" gets a traceable answer in the compliance matrix
- Page limits are hard limits — being over is a scored deduction in most
  public procurements and an unforced error in all of them
