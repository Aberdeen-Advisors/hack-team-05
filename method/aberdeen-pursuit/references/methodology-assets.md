## RFP Playbook - the 6-stage selection method

Use when the engagement is a software, platform, or vendor selection. Typical duration
6 to 12 months depending on complexity and scope.

| Stage | Objective | Key activities | Client-facing deliverable |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Preparation | Prepare for success | Define high-level scope, gather requirements, run an RFI, identify process challenges and capability improvements, create high-level implementation plan, develop change management strategy | Scope, requirements, RFI pack, stakeholder map, implementation preview |
| 2. Business Case Development | Develop benefit & cost model | Build business case model, identify B/S and P&L and qualitative benefits, initial high-level cost estimate, refine with vendor inputs, analyze attractiveness (ROI, payback), finalize | Business case model, cost estimate, ROI analysis |
| 3. Governance Model Development | Build strong governance | Align program sponsors, define decision rights (RACI), create initial governance model, use the selection process as a test run, finalize, prepare change and adoption | Governance model, decision-rights matrix |
| 4. Vendor Selection | Determine fitting vendor | Research vendors via third-party evaluators and reference calls, create and submit RFPs, analyze results, hold demos, weighted scoring, select | Weighted scorecard, evaluation readout, recommendation |
| 5. Finalize Scope / Resources / Time | Finalize due diligence | Finalize implementation scope, resources and timeline, check hidden and ongoing costs, clarify license and subscription counts | Final scope, master schedule, TCO |
| 6. Contract Negotiation | Finalize agreements | Finalize vendor agreements, negotiate legal terms with procurement and counsel, complete and sign SOWs | Executed agreements, SOWs |

**Distinctive elements worth naming in a proposal**, because they are what makes the
method defensible rather than generic:

- Effort is rated per activity for **both** Aberdeen and the client (High / Moderate /
  Low). Showing the client's own effort is a credibility signal.
- Vendor research uses third-party evaluators (Gartner, Info-Tech, Pathfinder) **plus**
  standardized peer reference calls, scored apples-to-apples.
- Scoring is weighted, with sensitivities run against alternative weightings so the
  recommendation is auditable rather than a beauty contest.
- Scorers score live, before discussing with peers, to preserve objectivity.
- The governance model is stress-tested by running the vendor selection through it before
  implementation begins.

## The offering set, and the scope boundary

Load the current service-offering matrix from the corpus before running the stage 1 scope-fit
test. Search `service offerings` in the Context folder; it is a workbook, and it is the
authoritative list of what Aberdeen sells.

It carries, per offering: the offering name, which of the 4 E's it sits under, what it
includes, the verticals it applies to, and its source. It also carries three sheets the
pursuit stages should use directly:

| Sheet | Use it for |
|---|---|
| Service Offerings | The scope-fit test in stage 1 step 5c. This list *is* the boundary. |
| Client Challenges, with "what to listen for" | Stage 2 win themes. It maps the language a client actually uses to the offerings that answer it, which is the fastest route from the RFP's own words to a grounded theme. |
| ICP & Buying Signals | The qualification checklist below, in its current form |

**The boundary is real and the tool must be willing to name it.** Aberdeen is a
technology-enabled business transformation firm. Requests that are substantially clinical,
substantially construction or facilities, substantially staffing-only, or otherwise centred
on work no offering covers are outside it, however attractive the client. The recorded
precedent: a healthcare pursuit was declined because the request was broader than what the
firm provides and the technology component Aberdeen owns was only one part of it. That is a
scope decision, not an ICP decision, and stage 1 step 5c exists to catch it on day one.

Do not stretch an offering to cover a gap. An offering described more broadly than the
workbook supports is a fabrication with extra steps.

## Qualification - the ICP checklist

From the Foundational Strategy. Stage 1 scores bid/no-bid against this.

**Target verticals:** Healthcare · Technology · Retail · CPG · Telecommunications ·
Private Equity and portfolio companies

**Size:** $50M to $5B+ revenue or AUM

**Signals:** active transformation roadmap · M&A activity · technology modernization

**Buying committee:** CIO, CTO, CGO, CFO (economic buyers); COO, Head of Strategy
(influencers)

**Qualification checklist:**
- Executive sponsorship
- Defined strategic initiative requiring structured execution
- Budget authority within 90 days
- Openness to governance and benefits tracking
- **Scope sits inside the offering set** (see the scope boundary above). This one is not in
  the Foundational Strategy's list, and it is the reason most attractive pursuits are
  actually declined.

**Target mix:** 70% ICP-aligned, 30% opportunistic. An opportunistic pursuit is not
automatically a no-bid. Say which of the two it is and why.

**Pain points Aberdeen is positioned against:** strategy-to-execution gap · stalled
programs · modernization risk · growth mandates · fragmented technology landscape ·
pressure to justify ROI on transformation.
