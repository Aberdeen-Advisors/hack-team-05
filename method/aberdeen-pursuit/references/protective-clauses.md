# Protective & Commercial Language

Source: Client Response & Deliverable Playbook v1.0, Section 6 and Appendix B.

**Principle:** protect the firm without reading like a contract in a pitch. Proposals
carry clear guardrails framed as *how we work*. The binding versions live in the SOW and
the MSA.

**Not legal advice.** These are drafting standards to protect scope and margin. Counsel
owns the final SOW and MSA terms, especially liability, indemnity, IP, and termination.
Never expand liability or indemnity language in a proposal - reference the MSA.

---

## Where each protection belongs

| Protection | Lives in | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Assumptions & client dependencies | Proposal + SOW | Sponsor, access, decision SLAs. Shifts delay risk to the client and caveats our findings. |
| Out of scope | Proposal + SOW | Fences the engagement. Anything extra becomes a paid change order. |
| Change control | Proposal + SOW | Documented, jointly approved changes. The mechanism that captures scope creep as revenue. |
| Acceptance | Proposal + SOW | Gate-based review with a deemed-accepted backstop so schedule and payment proceed. |
| Fees, NTE cap, expenses | Proposal + SOW | Fixed fee by deliverable. Not-to-exceed on extras. Capped, pre-approved expenses. |
| Off-ramp / termination for convenience | Proposal (light) + SOW/MSA | Gate-based exit, paid for work to date. Client-friendly and protective. |
| Background-IP carve-out | Proposal + SOW/MSA | Client owns deliverables. Aberdeen keeps its frameworks and templates and licenses them. |
| Liability cap, indemnity | **MSA only** | Risk allocation. Reference the MSA, never restate. |
| Confidentiality, data, PHI | NDA / BAA / MSA | Required before handling regulated or sensitive data. |

## Stage 3 checklist for Section 11

The proposal's "Assumptions, Dependencies & Terms" section must cover, at minimum:

- [ ] **Client dependencies** - named executive sponsor, stakeholder availability,
      access to systems and documentation, decision turnaround expectation
- [ ] **Assumptions** - the factual premises the scope and price rest on, stated so that
      if one proves false, the change-control mechanism applies
- [ ] **Out of scope** - explicit, with "unless separately contracted"
- [ ] **Change control** - written change order at agreed rates
- [ ] **Acceptance** - review window with a deemed-accepted backstop
- [ ] **Fees and expenses** - fixed fee by deliverable, NTE on extras, capped and
      pre-approved expenses
- [ ] **Off-ramp** - gate-based, paid to date (light touch in a proposal)
- [ ] **Background IP** - client owns deliverables, Aberdeen retains frameworks and
      licenses them
- [ ] **Validity period** for the proposal and its pricing
- [ ] **Conflict of interest** statement where relevant
- [ ] **Contracting vehicle** - willingness to execute the client's MSA, BAA, NDA as
      the RFP requires

Where a term depends on commercial judgment that is not in the corpus (rates, caps,
specific carve-outs), emit `[NEEDS INPUT]` with the decision needed and who should make
it. Do not invent a number.

## Two commercial shapes, and the engine must ask which

Verified against an executed Aberdeen SOW. **Not every engagement is fixed fee by deliverable,
and assuming it is produces the wrong document.**

| | **Deliverable-based** | **Time and materials** |
|---|---|---|
| Priced by | Deliverable, fixed fee | Role, hours, rate, with a not-to-exceed |
| Structure | Numbered deliverables D1..Dn with acceptance criteria | Phases with activities, and a role-by-phase fee table |
| Acceptance | Review window with a deemed-accepted backstop | Approval of submitted hours, usually per invoice period |
| Change control | Written change order against the deliverable set | Written change order against the NTE, plus a rule for reprioritisation |
| Protects margin via | Scope fence and out-of-scope | The NTE, the approval mechanism, and expense terms |

**Ask which shape applies before drafting.** An advisory or staff-augmentation engagement is
usually T&M. An assessment with defined outputs is usually deliverable-based. A hybrid prices
some phases each way.

**Acceptance criteria are not optional; they are shape-dependent.** On a T&M engagement,
requiring deliverable acceptance criteria produces criteria for deliverables that do not exist.
The equivalent control is the hours-approval mechanism, and it belongs in the terms section.

### Three weaknesses to look for in our own T&M terms

Found in a real executed SOW, so these are not hypothetical:

1. **A clause letting the client define scope unilaterally over time.** "Client will determine
   what activities are in scope from time to time" removes the scope fence entirely. Flag it.
2. **An NTE that a verbal instruction can move.** If revisions "confirmed verbally" can change
   the estimate, the cap is advisory. Change control must be written to be a control.
3. **"Not expected to exceed" instead of "shall not exceed."** The first is a forecast, the
   second is a cap. Know which one is being signed.

None of these are drafting errors to correct silently. Name them, say what the exposure is, and
route the decision to the relationship owner with counsel.

## Document type sets the register

From Playbook Section 2. Getting this wrong causes the two classic mistakes:
over-lawyering a proposal, or under-protecting a contract.

| Document | Register |
|---|---|
| **RFI** | "Tell us what's possible." Capability and point of view. No pricing, no commitment. |
| **RFQ** | "What's your price?" Scope is defined. Quote plus terms. |
| **RFP** | "Propose how you'd solve this." Approach, qualifications, team, price. |
| **Proposal** | Persuasive and descriptive. States assumptions, scope, pricing. **Not binding.** |
| **SOW** | Binding. Scope, deliverables, timeline, fees, acceptance, roles, off-ramps. |
| **MSA** | The rules of the relationship. Liability, indemnity, IP, termination, confidentiality. |
| **BAA / NDA** | Required before handling regulated or confidential data. |
| **Change Order** | A change to an active SOW's scope, schedule, or fees. |

Identify the document type in stage 1 and hold it through every later stage. Persuasion
and assumptions live in the Proposal. Binding scope and off-ramps live in the SOW. Legal
risk allocation lives in the MSA.
